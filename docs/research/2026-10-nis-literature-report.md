# Evidence-Based Opportunities to Improve easyNIS From Published HCUP National Inpatient Sample Studies
> Archived GPT deep-research output (October 2026). Inline citation markers from the export were removed; claims are unverified unless confirmed in `docs/IMPROVEMENT-ROADMAP.md` (see "Report corrections").

## Synthesis and current easyNIS baseline

The strongest opportunity is **not to add more statistical-model wrappers**. Across the NIS literature reviewed here, the recurring sources of avoidable error occur earlier and later in the workflow: defining and documenting cohorts, respecting year-specific coding and schema changes, constructing the survey design correctly, preserving denominator provenance, handling diagnosis position and procedure timing, distinguishing hospitalizations from patients, and producing disclosure-safe outputs. Those are precisely the areas where a package can reliably save analyst effort without making scientific choices on the analyst’s behalf. This conclusion is consistent with both HCUP's current NIS checklist and methodological audits showing persistent nonadherence to NIS-specific methodological standards in published research.

The highest-priority easyNIS work therefore appears to be: **independent statistical validation of `nis_design()`; a methodological preflight report; machine-readable cohort/code provenance; an explicit annual-schema compatibility layer; design-aware reviewer-request sensitivity workflows; stronger disclosure auditing; validation and clearer provenance for cost estimation; and complete synthetic teaching workflows separate from the completed HOCM study.** None of those proposals requires implementing the two deferred extensions named in the prompt. The completed HOCM myectomy-versus-alcohol-septal-ablation work is treated here only as an existing reference; its site was reachable only as a redirect in the research environment, so I did not infer undocumented features from it.

### Repository and documentation snapshot

The public [easyNIS repository](https://github.com/MSaadAsif/easyNIS) was inspected on **October 18, 2026**. The GitHub interface showed the default branch as `main` and 231 commits. The exact HEAD commit SHA was not recoverable through the accessible GitHub rendering, so this report does **not** claim a commit-pinned inspection. The published pkgdown documentation identifies the development version as **0.2.0.9000**, while the README gives **v0.2.0** as the pinned released version.

The requested `docs/IMPROVEMENT-ROADMAP.md` could not be retrieved at that public path on `main`; both the ordinary GitHub and raw-content attempts returned 404. That establishes only that the requested path was inaccessible during this review, **not** that no roadmap exists elsewhere in the repository or its history. Consequently, the two deferred backlog proposals in the prompt are treated as user-supplied historical/backlog information rather than as repository-verified current roadmap entries.

The current baseline is more capable than a simple NIS loader. The repository describes lazy DuckDB-backed opening of annual data, diagnosis and procedure flags, survey-design construction, labeling, charge/cost handling, suppression, study scaffolding, comorbidity support, and several audit/sensitivity helpers. The package explicitly says that accepted years are 2012 onward and that acceptance of a year does not mean every annual layout has independently been verified.

Several current development features already line up unusually well with published-paper friction. `nis_attrition()` applies cumulative criteria and reports remaining and dropped observations in unweighted and weighted form; missing values in a criterion are excluded.  `nis_audit_code_transition()` compares code-prefix groups by period and can calculate year-over-year changes, but separate calls remain necessary when the code system itself changes.  `nis_flag_dx_position()` addresses principal-versus-secondary diagnosis requirements, while `nis_flag_pr_timing()` links matched procedure slots to the numbered `PRDAY` fields and explicitly represents missing, sentinel, and otherwise indeterminate timing.

`nis_design()` is the highest-consequence current feature. It constructs year-scoped NIS survey identifiers and adds a zero-domain placeholder for hospital-years with no cohort discharge before creating the nested survey design. The documentation explicitly describes that placeholder approach as experimental. It also removes records with unusable `YEAR`, `HOSP_NIS`, `NIS_STRATUM`, or `DISCWT`, preserving information about the exclusion in `nis_excluded`. Its `annualize` behavior divides by the number of distinct years, and a partial period such as 2015 Q4 still counts as one year.  This combination is powerful but makes independent statistical validation the single most important engineering requirement identified in this review.

`nis_dx_sensitivity()` is also well targeted but deliberately limited: it compares principal-only, secondary-only, and any-position definitions and returns weighted/unweighted presentation counts and outcome point estimates, while warning that those output estimates are **not a substitute for design-based standard errors**.  That limitation directly motivates a design-aware sensitivity workflow rather than another regression wrapper.

Current suppression is useful but does not fully implement HCUP disclosure logic. `nis_suppress()` masks low-frequency cells and can propagate masking to an overall cell when a group cell is masked, but the documentation says derivable cells still require analyst review and secondary suppression is not a general solver.  HCUP's current NIS checklist discourages reporting cells based on 10 or fewer observations and also requires that reported cells be based on at least two hospitals.

Finally, `nis_cost()` presently distinguishes adjusted charges from estimated adjusted costs and expects a cost-to-charge ratio variable, but the function is explicitly experimental and carries annual-layout caveats, including for 2023.  `nis_new_study()` already provides the right architectural foothold for teaching and reproducible-project work by creating a Quarto-based study skeleton rather than merely returning an analysis object.

Several cross-paper extraction patterns are more important than any one clinical result.

First, **code lists are analysis inputs, not incidental documentation**. Rubin places its procedure definitions in a supplement; Wright places the full GAS definitions in Supplement 1; de Havenon places additional cohort and variable coding in Table S1; the coding-validity study makes consistency among codes its scientific question.  A reproducible easyNIS workflow therefore needs to know not merely that “ICD codes were used,” but the code system, code string/prefix semantics, valid years, diagnosis/procedure position, source citation, clinical concept, and whether definitions can overlap.

Second, **cohort ordering matters**. A transfer exclusion applied before an outcome definition is not equivalent to a late analytic filter; missing race excluded from a disparity estimand is not equivalent to coding it as “unknown”; restricting U07.1 to April–December 2020 is part of the estimand, not an implementation detail.  `nis_attrition()` addresses cumulative filters, but current workflows do not yet appear to persist a complete cohort specification with code provenance and validity periods.

Third, **“weighted” is insufficient documentation** for NIS inferential analysis. Rubin explicitly documents survey commands and trend weights, whereas several other accessible method sections mention national weights without enough detail in the article text to confirm strata, PSUs or subpopulation handling. HCUP explicitly instructs analysts to incorporate `NIS_STRATUM` and `HOSP_NIS` in variance estimation.  Software can make that omission harder by recording the realized design specification in report metadata.

Fourth, **missingness is often variable-specific and scientifically consequential**. Rubin excludes low-frequency missingness in several fields but retains missing race as a category; Wright includes missing values as unknown; de Havenon reports very large differential NIHSS missingness and changes the analytic presentation for that variable.  There is therefore no evidence-based case for an easyNIS “one-button missing-data method.” The useful package role is to quantify, stratify and document missingness before modeling.

## Methodological suitability against HCUP guidance

HCUP's current NIS checklist, updated in September 2026, should be treated as the governing methodological baseline for package guardrails. Its most important rules are unusually amenable to software checks.

**The unit is a hospitalization, not a person.** HCUP states that NIS contains inpatient stays and cannot be used to track patients across admissions or determine events before or after a stay.  This affects wording as well as modeling. Titles or manuscripts that casually convert weighted discharges into “patients” can imply a patient-level population that the design cannot identify. The Monlezun title is an especially visible example; Wright likewise reports “patients” while its source datasets are encounter-based administrative samples. That does not necessarily invalidate their in-hospital calculations, but an easyNIS manuscript/preflight layer should default to “hospitalizations” or “discharges” and require an explicit override for person-language.

**NIS cannot support longitudinal patient outcomes.** Readmission, time-to-event after discharge, recurrent hospitalization per person, and person-level before/after comparisons require a dataset with linkage such as NRD or another source. HCUP is explicit that patients cannot be tracked in NIS.  None of the selected studies needs a new easyNIS longitudinal-survival helper; such an abstraction would encourage unsupported estimands.

**National, regional and hospital-characteristic analyses are different from state and hospital-volume analyses.** HCUP states that the NIS is not designed for state-level estimates; the SID should be used for state-specific work.  More subtly, since the 2012 redesign the NIS samples discharges within participating hospitals at varying sampling rates, so sampled discharge counts cannot be treated as each hospital's annual total volume. HCUP specifically warns against post-2011 hospital-level totals.  This makes procedural-volume variables in post-2012 structural-heart analyses a high-value preflight target. Maraey's and Monlezun's analyses both surface procedural volume in post-2012 NIS research; unless the value comes from a legitimate externally supplied hospital attribute rather than a count of sampled NIS discharges, a hospital-volume interpretation is unsupported.  By contrast, the pre-2012 general-surgery disparity study illustrates why the warning must be year-sensitive rather than absolute.

**Complex-survey inference is mandatory for national standard errors.** HCUP instructs analysts to use the discharge weights and to incorporate `NIS_STRATUM` and `HOSP_NIS` for correct variance estimates. For multi-year analyses spanning the redesign, it instructs analysts to use `TRENDWT` for pre-2012 observations and `DISCWT` for 2012 onward.  Rubin is a strong example: it explicitly applied HCUP trend weights and survey commands before sending annual estimates and SEs into Joinpoint.  Studies whose articles merely say “nationally weighted” without reporting the complete design should be labeled **insufficiently reported**, not automatically incorrect.

This is also why easyNIS's experimental zero-domain placeholder strategy deserves independent validation before further convenience layers are built on top of it. `nis_design()` attempts to preserve hospital-year design information even when a study cohort has zero records in a hospital-year, which is exactly the sort of domain-analysis issue that can affect variance estimation. But the package documentation itself says the implementation is experimental and pending agreement checks against a full-data survey design.  A direct validation study is therefore a stronger next step than adding more model wrappers.

**Coding changes have two distinct dimensions: clinical code-system changes and NIS structural changes.** The October 1, 2015 ICD-9-CM-to-ICD-10-CM/PCS transition splits calendar year 2015, while the NIS sampling redesign occurred in 2012. HCUP warns that trend analyses spanning the ICD transition need explicit treatment of discontinuities and coding changes.  Wu's 1999–2018 IHCA study encounters both transitions; de Havenon's U07.1 analysis adds a different kind of validity period, because the COVID-19 code became available only in late March 2020.  This argues for a generic **code-validity interval** abstraction, not a hard-coded list of transition-specific clinical concepts.

easyNIS already has a sensible starting point: it treats 2015 Q1–Q3 as ICD-9 and Q4 as ICD-10, explicitly says it does not supply a clinical crosswalk, and has `nis_audit_code_transition()`.  The gap is that these facts are not yet unified into a study-level preflight that can say, for example, “your ICD-10 exposure is being applied to months where it cannot exist,” “your Q4-2015 cohort is being annualized as a full year,” or “your clinical definition changes code system here and no mapping source is recorded.”

**Secondary diagnoses are not automatically in-hospital complications.** HCUP cautions that secondary diagnoses generally cannot distinguish pre-existing comorbidity from a complication unless the code itself identifies an event occurring during the hospitalization or other timing information supports the interpretation.  This is a documentation/scientific-decision issue rather than a reason to ban secondary-diagnosis searches. de Havenon's I63-any-position criterion is a population definition, for example, not a claim that a secondary stroke necessarily developed after admission.  `nis_flag_dx_position()` and `nis_dx_sensitivity()` create exactly the right primitives; a preflight should add a warning when the analyst labels a generic secondary diagnosis as a “complication.”

**Procedure timing requires validity and missingness checks, not just date arithmetic.** HCUP's procedure-day variables vary in availability and include pre-admission values; the current `nis_flag_pr_timing()` correctly connects each matched procedure code to the correspondingly numbered `PRDAY` field and distinguishes missing/sentinel timing from known timing.  The literature shows why this matters: timing studies can change the cohort by excluding pre-admission procedures or categorizing same-day versus delayed treatment.  The missing piece is a year-by-year **timing completeness diagnostic** before a timing exposure is accepted.

**Charges and estimated costs must stay distinct.** The NIS contains hospital charge information, while cost analyses generally require cost-to-charge conversion and then, for real-dollar comparisons across years, an explicit inflation convention. easyNIS already preserves separate adjusted-charge and adjusted-cost outputs and requires `CCR_NIS`, but labels the implementation experimental.  Monlezun's resource-use analysis demonstrates why every cost table should carry provenance for the CCR source/year, inflation base year and missing-CCR handling rather than simply returning a column called “cost.”

**Disclosure is more than “n ≤ 10.”** Current HCUP guidance discourages cells based on 10 or fewer observations and requires at least two hospitals behind a reported cell.  Current `nis_suppress()` addresses the low-cell part and some simple secondary suppression but explicitly does not guarantee that all inferential disclosure is prevented.  That is a concrete software gap rather than a scientific judgment call.

The importance of these checks is not theoretical. Khera and colleagues' methodological assessment warned that NIS studies are susceptible to errors caused by misunderstanding the dataset and its changing design, and a broader review found substantial variation in cardiac NIS coding and low adherence to recommended NIS practices.  A 2026 follow-up in *JAMA Network Open* found that adherence to NIS methodological standards remained a contemporary problem rather than a resolved historical one.

| NIS survey object with weights/strata/clusters and domain | **A** | `nis_design()` creates the design, year-scopes hospital and stratum identifiers and records invalid design-row exclusions. Its zero-domain placeholder method remains experimental.  | Supported but validation gap |

| Survey means, totals, proportions, logistic/linear models and interactions | **B** | Once a valid survey design exists, these are ordinary survey-analysis operations. easyNIS already depends on `srvyr` and suggests `survey`; paper-specific regression choices should stay outside a bespoke wrapper.  | Ecosystem/statistical decision |

| Propensity weighting/matching and balance diagnostics | **B** | The easyNIS study template already points to established `WeightIt`/`cobalt` workflows; Monlezun illustrates propensity-adjusted comparative analysis. A new generic propensity wrapper would add little NIS-specific value.  | Ecosystem/statistical decision |
| Marginal effects / adjusted probabilities | **B** | de Havenon uses weighted logistic regression plus marginal effects. This is a model/reporting choice after design construction, not an NIS file-format problem.  | Ecosystem/statistical decision |

| Ordered cohort specification with source/version/validity provenance | **C** | Current flags and attrition cover execution but do not appear to encode a manuscript-ready provenance graph for code source, code-system validity, diagnosis position, overlap rules, transfer rules and missing-value policy. This recurs across almost every selected study. | Software/documentation gap |

Papers repeatedly require variants: diagnosis position, transfer exclusion, timing policy, overlapping procedures, transition-period exclusion.  | Software/reviewer-workflow gap |

Generic rule assertions would be reusable without embedding clinical code sets.  | Software/data-quality gap |
| Standard low-cell suppression | **A** | `nis_suppress()` masks small cells and has limited secondary behavior.  | Supported |
| Two-hospital contributor rule and systematic derivability audit | **C** | HCUP's disclosure requirement goes beyond the package's current low-count masking.  | Software/compliance gap |
| Charge inflation and CCR-based estimated cost | **A** | `nis_cost()` directly supports adjusted charges/costs using CCR information, but is explicitly experimental and carries annual assumptions.  | Supported but validation/documentation gap |
| Current `nis_comorbidity()` | **A** | The function exists and is documented as an experimental ICD-10 Elixhauser-related implementation using `medicalcoder`; it lacks POA use and is not claimed to reproduce HCUP/Stata-derived scores.  | Supported, experimental |
| Exact paper-specific AHRQ Elixhauser recreation | **D / deferred** | Rubin explicitly used HCUP software; package docs explicitly warn that the existing implementation is not expected to match historical HCUP/Stata outputs. A new AHRQ-specific implementation is deferred by the research brief.  | Deferred software requirement |
| Full pre-2012 NIS support | **D** | Current easyNIS scope begins at 2012. Several literature exemplars therefore cannot be fully reconstructed in easyNIS today. This is a real scope boundary, but the literature does not establish that broad pre-2012 support should outrank safer 2012+ improvements.  | Current scope/data complexity |
| NIS state estimates | **D** | HCUP says NIS should not be used for state-level estimates; SID is the appropriate source.  | Data limitation |

The most important interpretation of this matrix is that **A does not always mean “scientifically validated.”** In particular, `nis_design()`, `nis_cost()` and `nis_comorbidity()` are existing package capabilities whose own documentation contains experimental or verification caveats.  Conversely, **B does not mean “unimportant”**: propensity scores, interaction models, multiple imputation and marginal effects can be scientifically difficult, but their difficulty is not uniquely caused by NIS and therefore does not justify an easyNIS abstraction by itself.

## Prioritized improvement backlog

The ranking below weights five considerations qualitatively: recurrence across the study set, consequence of getting it wrong, repetitive analyst effort eliminated, implementation complexity, and strength of evidence. These are **proposals for evaluation**, not authorization to implement.

| Rank | Candidate | Why it ranks here | Proposed behavior and bounded next step |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Independent statistical validation of `nis_design()` and annualization** | Survey correctness affects virtually every national estimate in the study set, and HCUP explicitly requires correct weights, strata and clusters. The current zero-domain placeholder method is itself labeled experimental, so an undetected variance error could propagate across every downstream model. Rubin provides a strong reference workflow for survey estimates; the broader methodological literature shows that survey-design errors are a persistent NIS problem.  | Do **not** start with new functionality. Build a deterministic, nonpatient synthetic NIS-like fixture with multiple years, strata, hospital-years with zero domain observations, singleton conditions and invalid design rows. Compare easyNIS totals, means, ratios, regression coefficients, SEs, degrees of freedom and confidence intervals against a canonical full-data `survey` design followed by proper domain restriction. Include explicit tests for annualization and partial-year cohorts. Publish the validation protocol and expected tolerances. |
| **2** | **`nis_preflight()` methodological audit** | This addresses multiple high-consequence errors with relatively low scientific overreach: hospitalization-versus-patient wording, post-2011 hospital-volume calculations, state inference, invalid design fields, 2015 code transitions, partial-year annualization, PRDAY availability, charge/cost provenance and disclosure prerequisites. HCUP already supplies the rules; the package can operationalize them as warnings without choosing a model.  | Return a structured, printable report with `error`, `warning`, `information` and `not-checkable` statuses. First bounded version should inspect only objective metadata/rules: years present; design variables/invalid rows; ICD regime; whether a study period is partial; use of PRDAY; requested hospital-volume/state analyses when declared; cost/CCR metadata; and suppression prerequisites. It should never “approve” scientific validity. |
| **3** | **First-class cohort and code provenance** | Rubin, Wright, de Havenon, Mpody and the coding-validity study all show that reconstruction depends on *ordered* eligibility rules and code metadata, not simply a final Boolean cohort. Current flaggers and `nis_attrition()` execute pieces of this workflow but do not appear to produce a persistent machine-readable study definition.  | Introduce a lightweight provenance object rather than a new clinical ontology. Each criterion would store label, expression/function, source citation, code system, valid-from/valid-through dates when known, diagnosis/procedure position policy, missing-value policy and sequence. A renderer could produce a cohort-flow table and methods appendix. Start with existing flags/attrition and JSON/YAML serialization; do not curate new clinical code sets as part of this work. |
| **4** | **Explicit annual-schema and field-compatibility manifest for 2012+** | easyNIS presently accepts 2012+ while warning that annual layouts are not all independently verified; 2023 and CCSR assumptions are specifically flagged. Literature requirements include date-limited codes, varying PRDAY availability, ICD transition rules and changing variable names.  | Create a version-controlled manifest for *package-supported* years describing design fields, DX/PR slot patterns, PRDAY availability, charge-field naming, known ICD regime, and package verification status. `nis_open()`/preflight can compare actual columns to the manifest and distinguish “verified,” “supported with assumptions,” and “unknown.” Keep the bounded first phase at **2012 onward**; do not expand into wholesale pre-2012 support. |
| **5** | **Design-aware reviewer sensitivity workflow** | Published NIS analyses repeatedly need defensible alternative cohorts: principal versus any diagnosis, exclude transfers, change procedure-timing assumptions, split 2015 periods, handle overlapping procedures, or restrict to records with complete timing. Current `nis_dx_sensitivity()` is useful but explicitly lacks design-based uncertainty.  | Add a generic sensitivity-grid orchestrator that accepts named cohort variants and one analyst-supplied survey estimator/model function. It should return identical denominators, weighted estimates, confidence intervals, exclusions and provenance across variants. First implementation should extend diagnosis-position sensitivity to use `nis_design()` for uncertainty rather than add new regression methods. |
| **6** | **Disclosure audit beyond simple small-cell masking** | All publishable NIS tables face HCUP disclosure constraints. Current suppression handles ≤10-style masking but explicitly cannot guarantee nonderivability, while HCUP also requires at least two contributing hospitals.  | Add a read-only `nis_disclosure_audit()` that, where the design identifiers are available, reports unweighted cell counts, number of contributing hospitals and obvious one-dimensional derivability conflicts. Integrate warnings with `nis_suppress()` but **do not** begin with a general secondary-suppression optimization solver. That keeps scope bounded and avoids falsely promising complete disclosure protection. |
| **7** | **Validate and strengthen cost/charge provenance** | Resource-utilization papers such as the TAVR/cancer analysis need reproducible cost semantics. easyNIS already has the relevant function, so the next gain is validation and provenance rather than another helper.  | Validate `nis_cost()` against documented hand calculations/known fixtures for each supported year; require/report CCR field/source, base inflation year/index, original charge field, missing-CCR count and whether the result is nominal charge, inflation-adjusted charge, or estimated cost. Resolve 2023 assumptions before claiming that year verified. |
| **8** | **Complete, synthetic teaching workflows built on `nis_new_study()`** | The package already has a study scaffold, while the literature demonstrates several recurring NIS workflow archetypes that are hard to learn from isolated function examples. Teaching studies are explicitly in scope and can exercise the package without becoming new clinical research.  | Build three **synthetic/invented-data** end-to-end lessons: a national-trends workflow showing survey estimates and coding/change-point provenance; a disparities/domain workflow showing missingness and interactions; and a procedure-timing workflow showing PRDAY unknown/sentinel handling and sensitivity variants. Each should have expected outputs and automated validation. Keep the completed HOCM study separate rather than adapting or “extending” it. |

A few tempting ideas are intentionally **not** recommended.

Broad pre-2012 support would allow direct recreation of more historical trend papers, but it would substantially expand layout, weighting and survey-design complexity exactly when the 2012+ implementation still contains explicitly unverified components.  The evidence favors first making the current supported era auditable and validated.

Likewise, no dedicated Joinpoint, propensity-score, marginal-effects or multiple-imputation wrapper is recommended merely because papers use those methods. Rubin can export survey-aware annual estimates to specialized trend software; the current study template already points users toward established propensity/balance packages.  easyNIS adds the most value when it handles the NIS-specific parts that ordinary statistical packages do not know about.

The proposed preflight should also avoid becoming a mechanized peer reviewer. Whether a diagnosis belongs in the confounder set, whether complete-case analysis is defensible, whether a treatment comparison identifies a causal estimand, or whether an interaction belongs in the model remains a scientific decision. The package can expose denominators, missingness, survey structure and coding provenance; it should not silently decide the estimand.

## Deferred requirements note

Two recurrent literature requirements map directly to the extensions that this brief explicitly defers. They should be preserved as requirements only.

**Curated cardiac-device code sets.** Structural-heart, arrest and other cardiovascular papers repeatedly need versioned procedure/device definitions, sometimes with mutually overlapping procedures and time-dependent ICD-10-PCS codes. The requirements exposed by these papers are: source citation, ICD system, exact code or prefix semantics, valid dates, position/slot policy, overlap rules, and tests that detect conflicting categories.  Those requirements should inform future design of any curated code registry, but **`nis_codes_cardiac_device()` is deferred and is not part of the recommended backlog above**. The generic provenance and contradiction-audit proposals can proceed without adding curated device content.

**AHRQ Elixhauser computation.** Several studies explicitly use an Elixhauser score or HCUP comorbidity software. Rubin states that its comorbidity burden was calculated with HCUP software; Wright reports an Elixhauser index; structural-heart studies commonly use Charlson or Elixhauser-type adjustment.  The reproducibility requirement is therefore to record the exact comorbidity algorithm, software/version, code system, POA behavior where relevant, and whether an index or individual indicators were used.

The **existing** `nis_comorbidity()` remains assessable: public documentation describes it as experimental, ICD-10-oriented, implemented through `medicalcoder`, without POA use, and not expected to reproduce historical HCUP/Stata-derived Elixhauser values exactly.  That means a paper saying merely “Elixhauser” cannot safely be mapped to `nis_comorbidity()` without first determining which Elixhauser implementation it used.

The appropriate in-scope action is documentation/provenance: easyNIS should make the chosen comorbidity method conspicuous in the study manifest and prevent users from describing its existing output as an AHRQ reproduction unless independently established. A new **`nis_comorbidity_ahrq()` implementation is explicitly deferred and is not recommended for starting or scheduling here.**

## Unresolved questions and source-access limitations

The largest unresolved package question is **statistical equivalence of `nis_design()` to a conventional full NIS survey design followed by proper domain/subpopulation analysis**. The package documentation itself identifies the zero-domain placeholder variance method as experimental, and no independent validation artifact was found in the public documentation inspected here.  This uncertainty is consequential enough that it should be resolved before treating current easyNIS inferential results as a validated reference implementation.

The second is **annual layout verification**. The package says 2012+ is accepted but simultaneously identifies annual assumptions—especially 2023—and some CCSR layouts as unverified.  A supported-year manifest should distinguish “the loader accepts these files” from “every field used by this workflow has been verified against that year's HCUP layout.”

The third is **whether the package's current pre-2015 experimental ICD-9 support is intended to remain only a transitional convenience for 2012–2015 or to become broader historical-study support**. The selected literature demonstrates the research demand for 1990s/2000s trend work, but it does not establish that implementing the entire pre-2012 NIS era is a good near-term package investment. Given the sampling redesign and need for `TRENDWT`, it should remain an explicit current limitation unless separately justified.

The fourth is **procedure-day completeness**. `nis_flag_pr_timing()` has good semantics for missing and sentinel values, but a timing study needs to know whether missingness varies materially by year and procedure slot before converting that variable into an exposure. HCUP documents variation in PRDAY availability; no current easyNIS function located in the public docs produces that pre-analysis coverage profile.

The fifth is **cost-method validation**. `nis_cost()` has an appropriate conceptual separation between charges and CCR-derived estimated costs, but its documentation labels the method experimental.  Before expanding it, the package needs a year-specific validation record and explicit metadata about CCR and inflation assumptions.

Several literature details remain inaccessible rather than unknown in principle. Thomas and Canner are publicly indexed in PMC, but the research crawler repeatedly hit PMC's browser check when opening their detailed methods.  Mpody's publisher full text returned a payment-required response, although PubMed and indexed descriptions were accessible.  The Wright supplement link was visible but could not be resolved through the crawler; de Havenon's supplemental PDFs were identified, but they were not analyzed here, so this report does not pretend to have extracted their code tables.  Patel's primary JAMA Cardiology methods were likewise not directly recovered, so detailed eligibility/code/model fields for that row are deliberately marked unavailable rather than reconstructed from secondary citations.

The Adalbert publisher full-text page surfaced and the DOI was verified, but detailed methods were not retrieved before the browsing limit, so its exact years and contradiction rules remain an extraction gap.  The procedure-timing/general-surgery study was sufficiently indexed to establish its core timing and pre-2012 requirements but not to recover reliable DOI metadata; that field is therefore left unstated.

The requested roadmap presents a similar access limitation: `docs/IMPROVEMENT-ROADMAP.md` returned 404 at the attempted current public path.  The exact current Git commit SHA was also not exposed through the accessible GitHub rendering, although branch `main`, repository state and public documentation version were available.  These limitations are why this report distinguishes **released features**, **public development features**, **explicitly experimental assumptions**, **user-supplied deferred backlog items**, and **unverified assumptions** rather than collapsing all of them into a single “current feature list.”

Taken together, the literature and HCUP guidance point toward a coherent product direction: easyNIS should become less of a collection of convenience calls and more of a **verifiable NIS research protocol layer** around the existing R survey ecosystem. The package already has many of the right primitives—lazy loading, position-aware flags, PRDAY matching, attrition, transition audits, survey construction, suppression and study scaffolding.  The strongest evidence-based next work is to connect those primitives with provenance, preflight checks, annual compatibility metadata, design-based sensitivity reporting and independent validation, while leaving clinical code-set curation, paper-specific modeling choices, unsupported hospital/patient estimands, and the two explicitly deferred extensions outside the near-term scope.
